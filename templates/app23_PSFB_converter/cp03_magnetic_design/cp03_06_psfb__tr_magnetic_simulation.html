To perform the numerical simulations, the simulation conditions and models were first defined. It was assumed that the transformer design used the turns ratio ($ N = 8 $) and was implemented using planar technology.

While the winding structure and interconnection scheme differed between variants, a key design constraint was that the maximum magnetic flux density in the core did not exceed $125$ mT.

The operating conditions were summarized in Table 4.3.

The transformer was evaluated through numerical simulations in order to determine the key electrical and magnetic parameters, including:

  • Equivalent winding resistances at the operating frequency;
  • Winding losses and core losses;
  • Intrinsic leakage inductance;
  • Maximum magnetic flux density in the core.

Finite Element Method (FEM) simulations were carried out using Ansys Electromagnetics software suite.

For the FEM analysis, an adaptive discretization mesh was employed. This type of mesh increased resolution in regions with high magnetic field non-uniformity (e.g., air gaps) and applied a coarser mesh in areas where the magnetic field was relatively uniform. As a result, simulation time was significantly reduced without compromising result accuracy.

For the FEM simulation, a maximum error threshold of 2% was specified. This parameter, defined within the Ansys software, controlled the mesh refinement level required to ensure proper solution convergence.
